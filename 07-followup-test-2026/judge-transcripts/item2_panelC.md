# Evidence Assessment: Hydroxyethyl Starch (HES) for Fluid Resuscitation

## Claim under review
"Hydroxyethyl starch solutions are a safe and effective choice for fluid resuscitation in critically ill and surgical patients, with outcomes comparable to or better than crystalloid fluids."

## Final rating: **UNRELIABLE as a general claim** (weak-to-contradicted for critically ill patients; weak/conflicted for surgical patients)

The claim, stated as a general proposition covering both critically ill and surgical patients, is contradicted by the preponderance of large, independent, publicly funded randomized trials and by unanimous regulatory action in the US, EU, and UK. The evidence base is unusual among "settled" clinical questions in that (a) it was substantially poisoned by one of the largest research fraud cases in anesthesiology history, (b) after removing the fraudulent data the independent evidence still shows harm, and (c) the most recent large-scale evidence base favoring the claim — for the *surgical* subgroup only — is industry-funded by the leading HES manufacturer, with the study's own authors holding financial ties to fluid manufacturers. This is a textbook case where "safe and effective" language does not survive contact with the primary literature.

---

## 1. The critically ill / sepsis population: evidence of harm is strong, consistent, and independent

Three large, publicly/academically funded multicenter RCTs directly tested HES against crystalloid or lower-risk comparators in critically ill and septic patients, all with mortality or renal outcomes as primary endpoints:

- **VISEP (Brunkhorst FM, Engel C, Bloos F, et al.)** "Intensive Insulin Therapy and Pentastarch Resuscitation in Severe Sepsis." *N Engl J Med.* 2008;358:125–139. A 2×2 factorial trial (German Competence Network Sepsis) comparing 10% pentastarch (HES 200/0.5) vs. modified Ringer's lactate in severe sepsis. Stopped early for safety; HES arm showed more acute renal failure and renal-replacement therapy.
  https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa070716

- **CRYSTMAS (Guidet B, et al.)** "Assessment of hemodynamic efficacy and safety of 6% hydroxyethylstarch 130/0.4 vs. 0.9% NaCl fluid replacement in patients with severe sepsis: the CRYSTMAS study." *Crit Care.* 2012;16:R94.

- **6S trial (Perner A, Haase N, Guttormsen AB, et al.)** "Hydroxyethyl Starch 130/0.42 versus Ringer's Acetate in Severe Sepsis." *N Engl J Med.* 2012;367:124–134. Scandinavian Critical Care Trials Group, 798 patients with severe sepsis. Primary outcome: death or end-stage kidney failure at 90 days. **Result: 201/398 (51%) died in the HES arm vs. 172/400 (43%) in the Ringer's-acetate arm (RR 1.17, 95% CI 1.01–1.36, P=0.03)** — a statistically significant mortality increase with HES.
  https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1204242

- **CHEST trial (Myburgh JA, Finfer S, Bellomo R, et al.)** "Hydroxyethyl Starch or Saline for Fluid Resuscitation in Intensive Care." *N Engl J Med.* 2012;367:1901–1911. The largest trial in this space: 7,000 general ICU patients randomized to 6% HES 130/0.4 (Voluven) vs. 0.9% saline for all resuscitation needs, with 90-day all-cause mortality as the primary endpoint. **Result: no significant mortality difference (RR ~1.06, not significant), but significantly more patients in the HES arm required renal-replacement therapy (RR 1.21) and had persistently elevated creatinine.**
  https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1209759

These are large, adequately powered, peer-reviewed, publicly funded RCTs — the strongest tier of primary evidence available on this question — and none showed a mortality or renal benefit for HES; two showed significant harm (renal failure/RRT, and in 6S, mortality itself).

## 2. Meta-analyses, including one that had to be redone after a fraud scandal

- **Zarychanski R, Abou-Setta AM, Turgeon AF, Houston BL, McIntyre L, Marshall JC, Fergusson DA.** "Association of Hydroxyethyl Starch Administration With Mortality and Acute Kidney Injury in Critically Ill Patients Requiring Volume Resuscitation: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis." *JAMA.* 2013;309(7):678–688.
  38 RCTs, 10,880 patients. Most trials (95%) had unclear-to-high risk of bias. **After excluding data fabricated by Joachim Boldt, HES was associated with a significant increase in mortality (RR 1.09, 95% CI 1.02–1.17) and renal failure (RR 1.27, 95% CI 1.09–1.47).**
  https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/1653505

- **Cochrane reviews:**
  - Mutter TC, Ruth CA, Dart AB. "Hydroxyethyl starch (HES) versus other fluid therapies: effects on kidney function." *Cochrane Database Syst Rev.* 2013;CD007594.
  - Perel P, Roberts I, Ker K. "Colloids versus crystalloids for fluid resuscitation in critically ill patients." *Cochrane Database Syst Rev.* 2013 (pub6); updated 2018 (pub7), CD000567.
  Cochrane's consistent conclusion: no mortality benefit from HES/colloids over crystalloids, and increased risk of adverse renal outcomes and need for RRT with HES.

## 3. The Boldt fraud scandal — a direct threat to the integrity of this evidence base

**Joachim Boldt**, a leading German anesthesiologist and prominent HES advocate, was found to have fabricated data on a massive scale:
- 88–103 of his ~102 published studies were retracted (many for fabricated data and/or missing IRB/ethics approval).
- Described by commentators as comparable in scale to the Andrew Wakefield and Yoshitaka Fujii misconduct cases.
- Boldt's papers had directly informed earlier positive assessments of HES safety in surgical/cardiac-surgery contexts.
- When Zarychanski et al. (JAMA 2013, above) and other reviewers re-ran their analyses **excluding** Boldt's data, the harm signal for mortality and renal failure not only persisted but strengthened, meaning the original pooled evidence was not merely weakened by fraud — the fraud had been masking harm.

Sources: Wikipedia "Joachim Boldt"; "Fraud in anaesthetic research and publication," PMC3327055; RetractionWatch coverage (2013, 2015); Science.org coverage of the case.

## 4. Regulatory history — a near-decade-long escalation, ending in EU market suspension

- **2013:** FDA adds boxed warning: do not use HES in critically ill adults, including sepsis patients; avoid in pre-existing renal dysfunction; discontinue at first sign of renal injury; RRT reported up to 90 days post-administration. Same year, EMA's PRAC recommends suspending HES marketing authorizations EU-wide after CHEST, 6S, and CRYSTMAS data showed increased kidney injury/RRT risk in severe sepsis; UK's MHRA independently withdrew HES. Full EU suspension was delayed by manufacturer appeal; a restricted-use compromise (contraindications, warnings) was adopted instead in Dec 2013/Mar 2014.
  https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/hydroxyethyl-starch-solutions-infusion-be-suspended-cmdh-endorses-prac-recommendation

- **2018:** A third EMA review further restricts HES to accredited hospitals with mandatory clinician training (controlled access programme), given persistent off-label use in sepsis/critically ill/renal-impaired patients.

- **2021 (July):** FDA **expands** its warning beyond critically ill/septic patients to **surgical and blunt trauma patients**, citing new RCT and observational data showing increased mortality, acute kidney injury, and excess bleeding in surgical patients, and increased mortality/AKI in blunt trauma patients. FDA contraindicates use for hypovolemia "unless adequate alternative treatment is unavailable."
  https://pubs.asahq.org/anesthesiology/article/136/5/868/135838

- **2022:** A mandated post-2018 drug-utilization study shows HES **continues** to be used outside approved restrictions in vulnerable/contraindicated populations. PRAC concludes the 2018 risk-minimization measures failed, and that "benefits no longer outweigh risks." CMDh endorses suspension; **HES solutions for infusion are suspended from the EU market.**
  https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/prac-recommends-suspending-hydroxyethyl-starch-solutions-infusion-market-0

No major regulator has reversed course toward a "safe and effective, comparable-or-better-than-crystalloid" position. If anything, regulatory scope has only broadened over time (2013 sepsis/ICU → 2021 surgical/trauma → 2022 full EU suspension).

## 5. The surgical-patient subgroup: genuinely more contested, but the pro-HES evidence has a conflict-of-interest problem

This is the one place the underlying literature is less one-sided, and it deserves a fair hearing:

- **Van der Linden P, et al. / broader 2021 meta-analysis (Perioperative Medicine, 2021)**: 25 RCTs, 4,111 surgical patients, HES 130/0.4. No statistically significant difference in 30-day mortality (RR 1.28, 95% CI 0.88–1.86), AKI (RR 1.23, 0.99–1.53), or RRT need (RR 0.75, 0.37–1.53) — but authors explicitly declined to recommend routine HES use given the trend toward harm and absence of confirmed benefit.
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13741-021-00182-8

- **Chappell D, et al.** *Br J Anaesth.* 2021;127:556–568 — tetrastarches in surgery/trauma; found no meaningful renal differences but noted few trials assessed long-term renal function.

- **FLASH trial** (high-AKI-risk abdominal surgery, N=775): found a **higher** incidence of postoperative AKI with HES (RR 1.34, 95% CI 1.00–1.80) — a signal of harm in a targeted high-risk surgical population.

- **PHOENICS trial** (N=1,958): found HES non-inferior to crystalloid for renal function at 90 days/1 year — a signal of safety in a broader, lower-risk surgical population.

- **Sessler DI, Pradelli L, Povero M, Chappell D, Wang E.** "Hydroxyethyl Starch and Perioperative Complications: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis." *Anesthesiology.* 2026;145(3):591–603. The largest and most recent meta-analysis (114 trials, 2009–2026, 13,951 surgical patients, 6,753 on HES 130/0.4). Found **no** increased AKI risk (RR 1.02, 95% CI 0.91–1.16), no worsening of creatinine, similar RRT rates, and no mortality difference at any timepoint up to 90 days — the most favorable large-scale HES data available.
  **Critical caveat: this study was funded by Fresenius-Kabi**, the manufacturer of Voluven/Volulyte (leading HES products), and two authors (Pradelli, Povero) received direct project funding from Fresenius Kabi while a third (Chappell) discloses lecture fees from multiple fluid manufacturers. This is a material conflict of interest on the single most favorable and most recent data point for the claim.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13460315/

Net read on surgical patients: independent trials are mixed (PHOENICS favorable, FLASH unfavorable in a high-risk subgroup), independent meta-analyses (2021) found no significant harm but explicitly withheld a recommendation to use HES, and the most favorable, largest, most recent meta-analysis is industry-funded. Meanwhile the FDA moved in the opposite direction in 2021, expanding its warning to cover surgical patients specifically for mortality, AKI, *and* excess bleeding — based on RCT and observational data the agency found compelling enough to act on. The earlier 2013 FDA warning was itself partly grounded in an 18-RCT meta-analysis showing increased surgical bleeding with HES regardless of molecular weight/substitution degree.

## 6. Bottom line by sub-claim

| Sub-claim | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| HES "safe" in critically ill/septic patients | **Contradicted** | 6S (mortality ↑), CHEST (RRT ↑), VISEP (renal failure ↑), Zarychanski JAMA meta-analysis (mortality RR 1.09, renal failure RR 1.27), Cochrane reviews, and full EU market suspension (2022) |
| HES "effective/better than crystalloid" in critically ill | **Not supported** | No trial shows a mortality or major morbidity benefit; CHEST/6S/VISEP all null-to-harmful on primary endpoints |
| HES "safe and effective" in surgical patients | **Weak/contested, conflicted evidence** | Independent 2021 meta-analysis: no significant harm but no recommendation to use; FLASH (high-risk subgroup): harm signal; PHOENICS: reassuring; the only strongly favorable large-scale evidence (Sessler 2026) is industry-funded by the main HES manufacturer; FDA moved to expand (not relax) warnings for this population in 2021, citing bleeding, AKI, and mortality signals |
| Overall claim as stated (blanket "safe and effective...comparable to or better than crystalloid" across both populations) | **Unreliable** | Overstates a body of evidence that regulators worldwide have read in the opposite direction; ignores a major fraud scandal that originally inflated HES's safety profile; treats a genuinely contested surgical literature (with an unresolved industry-funding confound) as settled fact, and simply misstates the critically-ill literature, which is not contested |

## Why this rates "unreliable" rather than merely "weak"

A "weak" rating would apply to a claim resting on thin, small, or underpowered evidence with no clear directional signal. This claim is different: it is contradicted by large, adequately powered, independent RCTs on the primary (critically ill) population it invokes, it was historically propped up by outright data fabrication that regulators and meta-analysts had to explicitly strip out, and even in the one subgroup (surgical patients) where the picture is genuinely more mixed, the strongest evidence in the claim's favor comes from an industry-funded study whose authors have financial ties to HES manufacturers — while the independent evidence in that same subgroup stops short of endorsing routine use and the primary regulator (FDA) moved to expand warnings rather than relax them. Taken as a whole, an unqualified "safe and effective, comparable-or-better-than-crystalloid" framing is not a defensible reading of this evidence base.
