# Fact-check: Arginine/omega-3-enriched perioperative immunonutrition and infectious complications after GI cancer surgery

**Claim:** "Arginine/omega-3-enriched perioperative immunonutrition formulas significantly reduce infectious complications after gastrointestinal cancer surgery."

**Verdict: TRUE, with moderate confidence and important caveats.**

The claim is supported by the weight of current evidence — including multiple independent 2024–2025 systematic reviews/meta-analyses and the ESPEN clinical practice guideline — but the certainty of the underlying evidence is only low-to-moderate (GRADE), and a well-documented strand of methodologically rigorous literature shows the effect shrinks or disappears once industry-funded and high-risk-of-bias trials are excluded, and does not clearly hold in modern minimally invasive surgery. So "significantly reduce" is true as a description of the pooled statistical literature and guideline consensus, but it should not be read as a settled, bias-free, uniformly robust effect.

## Reasoning and evidence

### 1. The consistent, positive signal (majority of recent meta-analyses)

Multiple independent systematic reviews and meta-analyses published in 2024–2026, covering thousands of patients across dozens of RCTs, consistently report that immunonutrition formulas built on arginine + omega-3 fatty acids (usually also with nucleotides/RNA) reduce infectious complications after GI cancer surgery:

- **Large RCT meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42024524537; ~90 RCTs screened, 55 pooled, 7,462 patients, PMC13384005 / ScienceDirect S2161831326001043):** Arginine/nucleotide/omega-3 immunonutrition significantly reduced anastomotic leak (OR 0.62, 95% CI 0.50–0.76), respiratory infection (OR 0.46, 95% CI 0.33–0.64), UTI (OR 0.58, 95% CI 0.38–0.89), wound infection (OR 0.67, 95% CI 0.46–0.98), and sepsis (OR 0.45, 95% CI 0.28–0.70), and shortened hospital stay by ~2.47 days. Notably, **omega-3 fatty acids given alone did not** produce these benefits — the effect appears to require the combined formula (arginine + omega-3 + nucleotides), not omega-3 in isolation. Certainty of evidence for these outcomes was rated **moderate** by GRADE, with the authors flagging inconsistency, indirectness, and possible publication bias. No conflicts of interest were declared for this analysis.

- **Upper GI cancer meta-analysis (23 studies; PubMed 38474706 / PMC10933766, Annals of Surgery 2024):** Immunonutrition reduced infectious complications versus standard nutrition (RR 0.72, 95% CI 0.57–0.92), roughly a 30% relative reduction, certainty of evidence rated **moderate**.

- **Colorectal cancer surgery meta-analysis (2025; 9 studies, 1,198 patients, PMC13333496 / PubMed 42440717):** Enteral immunonutrition reduced infectious complications (OR 0.48, 95% CI 0.34–0.66, p<0.001), though it did **not** significantly reduce anastomotic leak, ileus, or length of stay — showing the benefit is fairly specific to infection outcomes in this subgroup.

- **TROGSS umbrella review (2025, PMC12300233):** Across an umbrella of prior meta-analyses in GI oncology, arginine/glutamine/omega-3 formulations were associated with reduced wound infection and improved recovery; non-infectious complications also reduced (RR 0.83, 95% CI 0.75–0.92).

- **Umbrella review of interventional/observational meta-analyses (PMC13543360):** Enteral immunonutrition in GI cancer surgery (3,692 patients pooled) associated with significantly lower overall complications, infectious complications, surgical site infection, anastomotic leak, SIRS duration, and antibiotic-therapy duration.

### 2. Guideline endorsement

The **ESPEN practical guideline on clinical nutrition in surgery** (2021, updated 2025; Weimann et al., *Clinical Nutrition*) recommends peri-/postoperative enteral formula enriched with arginine, omega-3 fatty acids, and ribonucleotides for malnourished patients undergoing major cancer surgery (**Grade B** recommendation), and preoperative oral immunonutrition for 5–7 days preoperatively (Grade 0/GPP). The guideline cites a meta-analysis (Hegazi et al.) showing a significant reduction in infectious complications specifically when immunonutrition is compared against a non-supplemented standard diet (OR 0.49, 95% CI 0.30–0.83) — an important nuance, since the effect is less clear when the comparator is itself a standard (non-immune) oral nutritional supplement rather than no supplement.

### 3. Important caveats that prevent an unqualified "TRUE"

- **Industry funding / publication bias:** A widely cited meta-analysis in the *British Journal of Surgery* (Probst et al.) found a stark split by funding source: non-industry-funded trials showed **no significant benefit** for overall complications (OR 1.13, 95% CI 0.88–1.46, p=0.34), while industry-funded trials showed large benefits (OR 0.66, 95% CI 0.48–0.91, p=0.01). Publication bias was statistically detected for infectious complications (p=0.002). When trials at high/unclear risk of bias were excluded, the pooled benefit for overall complications, infectious complications, and length of stay **disappeared**.

- **Surgical approach matters:** A 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis of immunonutrition in **minimally invasive** major abdominal surgery (11 RCTs, 890 patients) found immunonutrition had little-to-no effect on overall or infectious complications (GRADE: very low certainty) and did not reduce mortality (GRADE: low), concluding that benefits seen in open surgery "are not present in minimally invasive major abdominal surgery, making its routine use in this setting unjustified." This matters because an increasing share of GI cancer resections today are laparoscopic/robotic.

- **Heterogeneity:** Across reviews, formulas, dosing windows (preop-only vs. perioperative vs. postoperative), comparator arms (no supplement vs. standard ONS), and cancer types (gastric, colorectal, esophageal, pancreatic, hepatic) vary substantially, and effect sizes vary by outcome subtype (e.g., colorectal-specific data show infection benefit but not anastomotic-leak or LOS benefit).

- **Certainty of evidence:** Even the positive pooled analyses generally rate the evidence as **moderate at best**, and in some subgroup analyses as low or very low, reflecting risk-of-bias concerns, inconsistency, and indirectness rather than high-confidence causal proof.

## Bottom line

The claim is essentially **TRUE as a summary of the mainstream literature and current clinical guidelines**: across the majority of contemporary (2024–2026) meta-analyses and the ESPEN surgical nutrition guideline, arginine/omega-3-enriched perioperative immunonutrition formulas are associated with statistically significant reductions in infectious complications after GI cancer surgery, typically in the 25–50% relative-risk-reduction range, most robustly in **open, malnourished-patient, major cancer surgery** settings. However, this should be treated as **moderate-certainty, not settled evidence**: a meaningful part of the effect appears attributable to industry-sponsored and higher-risk-of-bias trials, the benefit is much weaker or absent for omega-3 given alone, is not clearly present in minimally invasive surgery, and disappears in bias-adjusted subgroup analyses in at least one major reanalysis. A fair characterization is "probably true, moderate confidence, with a credible risk that the true effect is smaller than the pooled literature suggests."
